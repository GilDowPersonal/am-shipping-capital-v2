Am Shipping Capital · The AI concept

An intelligent layer for shipping operations.

Less re-keying, clearer information, and agents that work for the team — never instead of it.

View the eight applications How it connects

Three terms

What we mean by each word.

Automation
Follows fixed rules, the same way every time.
AI
Makes sense of unstructured information such as emails, scans and free text.
Agent
Coordinates a bounded sequence of steps, each carried out with an approved tool.

None of them replaces accountable human judgment.

Everything below is a proposal to evaluate — not an installed system and not a verified capability. The company's real processes, software and data access would all need to be confirmed before any of it could be built.

Leg 1 · Before the shipment

A cleaner start.

  1. 01

    Application 01 · Document intelligence

    Paperwork in, usable data out.

    Booking requests, commercial invoices, packing lists and bills of lading arrive in every format imaginable. This assistant would lift out the references, cargo descriptions, weights and dates, compare the fields against each other, point out anything missing, and prepare a structured draft record that links back to the original document.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • An approved mailbox or document store
    • OCR with field-level validation
    • A draft record in the TMS (transport management system)

    A person decides

    • An operator reviews any uncertain extraction or mismatch before a record is committed

    Safeguard: the text inside a document is handled as data to be read — never as instructions to follow.

  2. 02

    Application 02 · Inquiry & quotation assistant

    A complete brief before the quote.

    When an inquiry comes in, the assistant would work out the route and what the cargo needs, then draft the questions that fill any gaps. It would also bring approved, current rates into a worksheet that spells out assumptions, validity dates and exclusions — so the person pricing starts from a full picture.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Email
    • The CRM
    • An approved source of rates

    A person decides

    • Pricing and terms
    • Every quote that leaves the company

    Safeguard: if a rate is missing or out of date, the request goes to the commercial team rather than to a model's guess. The assistant cannot reserve capacity or commit the company to anything.

Leg 2 · During the journey

See the change. Support the response.

  1. 03

    Application 03 · Shipment visibility

    Every milestone, one timeline.

    Carrier and terminal events would be collected, matched to the right shipment references and laid out as a single timestamped timeline. Data that has gone stale, milestones that were missed and arrival estimates that the source has changed would all stand out.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Licensed carrier and terminal APIs
    • EDI messages
    • The TMS, where available

    A person decides

    • Which updates matter
    • What customers are told

    Safeguard: source timestamps are preserved, duplicates removed and estimates labelled as estimates. A feed that is down is shown as unavailable — the system never makes up a location or promises an arrival time.

  2. 04

    Application 04 · Exception coordination

    Every exception gets an owner.

    When a milestone slips or a document goes missing, an agent would collect the facts, open a case, send it to the right team and attach a checklist of next steps. It would also draft a customer update and set reminders so the follow-up does not fall through the cracks.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Shipment events and approved rules
    • A case management tool
    • The internal task queue

    A person decides

    • An operations lead approves any rerouting, booking, extra charge or external message

    Safeguard: if a tool fails or sources disagree, the agent stops and escalates to a person with the evidence attached.

Leg 3 · People & knowledge

An informed team. A clearer conversation.

  1. 05

    Application 05 · Customer communication assistant

    Updates that are easier to write.

    The assistant would sum up where a shipment stands, draft a reply in plain language and translate it when needed. It would only draw on records the person using it is allowed to see, and it would show where each operational statement came from and how recent it is.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Shipment lookup filtered by permissions
    • The CRM
    • The helpdesk

    A person decides

    • Staff review and send every draft
    • Complaints, financial commitments and unclear answers are handled by a person

    Safeguard: the customer's identity and right to see a shipment are confirmed before any information is retrieved.

  2. 06

    Application 06 · Internal knowledge assistant

    Find the procedure, not the email thread.

    Staff could ask for approved procedures, document requirements or handover instructions and get an answer with links to the exact passages it relies on. When the library does not cover a question, the assistant would say so plainly.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • A versioned, access-controlled knowledge library
    • Retrieval that stays grounded in its sources

    A person decides

    • Compliance specialists own regulatory interpretation, dangerous goods and customs submissions

    Safeguard: customer records are kept apart from general procedures, and an answer is never a clearance or legal advice.

Leg 4 · The commercial picture

Know what needs a closer look.

  1. 07

    Application 07 · Invoice reconciliation

    Check the charge against the deal.

    Supplier invoices would be checked against the approved quotes, the bookings and what actually happened to the shipment. Duplicates, surprise surcharges, currency differences and contested storage or detention charges would be flagged for review.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Invoice intake
    • Approved contracts
    • The TMS
    • The accounting system, in read-only or draft mode

    A person decides

    • Finance resolves discrepancies
    • Finance approves posting and payment

    Safeguard: the agreed calculation rules and exchange-rate dates are recorded with every check, and the system cannot change bank details or release money.

  2. 08

    Application 08 · Operations intelligence

    A weekly report worth reading.

    A regular summary would cover open exceptions, the backlog of responses, document problems that keep coming back and costs drawn from approved records. Every observation would link down to the data behind it.

    Connections & human checkpoints

    Connects to

    • Approved reporting views from the TMS, CRM and finance systems

    A person decides

    • Management checks the interpretation before any change to routes, staffing or commercial policy

    Safeguard: the numbers come from defined queries and the AI only explains them. Each report states its coverage, its missing values and the period it describes.

How it connects

Plugged into the workflow, not bolted on.

The starting point is the set of tools people already use. Before choosing any model or agent framework, the real system landscape and who is allowed to access what would need to be confirmed.

  1. Receive

    An inquiry lands in an approved mailbox. The original message and its attachments are stored and matched to an authorised customer.

  2. Interpret & validate

    AI extracts the details; deterministic rules check fields and units. Anything with low confidence becomes a review task.

  3. Act through narrow tools

    A bounded agent reads only the records it is permitted to see and prepares a draft through an API, webhook, EDI or controlled import — with duplicate protection and an audit trail.

  4. Approve & log

    A named person approves the specific write or message. The decision is logged, and failures land in a queue everyone can see.

Controls by design

  • Each agent gets the least privilege it needs.
  • Credentials stay out of prompts.
  • Data is encrypted.
  • Retention periods are agreed in advance.
  • A provider's data-use terms are reviewed before any data is shared.
  • Emails, documents and feeds are treated as untrusted: they cannot grant permissions or authorise bookings, payments or disclosures.
  • Approval gates are enforced in the application, not in the prompt.
  • Sources, tool calls, reviews and outcomes are all traceable.
  • Missing data and failures are shown openly and handed to a person.

Nothing here is connected. This site explains proposed workflows; it does not access company systems, process real shipment data, or run agents.

A sensible first step

Prove one workflow first.

Begin with inquiry and document intake in draft-only mode, using approved, representative records and a review queue for operators.

  1. 01

    Map & baseline

    Name a process owner, agree the access scope, gather representative cases, and record how long handling takes today and what counts as an error.

  2. 02

    Run alongside the team

    Keep every output separate from production decisions. Measure extraction accuracy, missed issues, review effort and handling time against the baseline.

  3. 03

    Review before expanding

    Agree acceptance criteria with the team, investigate every failure and rehearse switching the system off. Narrowly permitted actions are added only after sign-off.

This is a proposal, not a commitment. No savings are promised before anything has been measured.
